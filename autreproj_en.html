DCR Solutions

Energy and computing

Where the idea started

In 2021 I started building machines to mine Ethereum. I had to choose components, monitor temperatures and find settings that would stay stable. A machine could run faster and still cost too much to operate. Later, renting out GPUs introduced another question: who needs this computing capacity, for what kind of work, and for how long?

Bringing computing to undervalued electricity

With DCR Solutions I’m exploring what happens when a modular data centre is installed near a producer with surplus or undervalued electricity. The machines use that power on site and businesses buy computing time. Solar installations offer promising opportunities. For each site, I also need to understand when power is available and how to provide a continuous service.

What I learned in the field

I spoke with more than fifteen people. I asked industrial users what work they would entrust to the machines. With energy professionals, I explored the terms under which a site’s electricity could be used. Financiers helped me identify which assumptions and supporting documents they would need to assess the project.

I built a financial forecast, compared the offer with existing computing services and obtained a first letter of intent. The next step is to match real site data to an actual user’s workload: how much power is available, what machine would be useful and how often they would use it.
